# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Three roles. **Primary**: receptionist at the garage front desk — interrupted workflow, customers standing nearby, phone calls happening. They create job cards, look up vehicles, generate invoices, and handle walk-ins. Speed and legibility dominate everything. **Secondary**: manager or owner reviewing daily performance analytics, typically at a desk without interruption. **Tertiary**: admin managing multiple garage locations.

## Product Purpose

Pitstop is a garage ERP for automotive service businesses operating in the UAE. It handles job intake, invoicing, quotations, inventory, and performance visibility. Success for the receptionist: open a job card in under 20 seconds and hand an invoice to a waiting customer without hunting through menus. Success for the manager: understand the shop's health in under 5 seconds on the dashboard.

## Brand Personality

Precise, composed, reliable. Feels like a premium tool built for professionals, not a consumer app designed to impress during a demo. The system projects confidence through clarity — structure and space do the work, not decorative chrome. Voice is direct: no "Welcome to Pitstop!" banners, no celebrations, no empty motivational copy.

## Anti-references

No hero-metric templates (giant number, tiny label, gradient accent card). No identical card grids repeating the same icon-heading-number layout endlessly. No neon-on-black or gaming aesthetic. No glassmorphism used decoratively. No generic SaaS gradient dashboard (purple blobs, glowing orbs). No flat enterprise gray (Microsoft/SAP-style with zero craft). No startup-minimal cream (Notion clones with off-white backgrounds and a single sans).

## Design Principles

1. **Clarity over ceremony** — every element earns its place. Decorative layers, gradient accents on inert cards, and repeated headings that restate the section are all dead weight.
2. **Speed reads** — the receptionist must extract shop state in 2 seconds. Layout and typographic hierarchy are the primary affordances. Color is a signal, not a theme.
3. **Restraint on accent** — the accent appears at decision points: primary action, active selection, alert. Not on background shapes, not as decoration, not on neutral cards.
4. **Light-native, dark-quality** — light mode is default (matching bright garage showrooms). Dark mode is equally polished, not an afterthought.
5. **Tool-grade, not app-store-grade** — forms and tables are the product. Avoid burying them behind hero sections or decorative chrome that slows the primary task.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Text contrast ≥4.5:1 for body, ≥3:1 for large text. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Status must never be conveyed by color alone (use shape + text alongside). Layout should anticipate RTL for future Arabic localization.
